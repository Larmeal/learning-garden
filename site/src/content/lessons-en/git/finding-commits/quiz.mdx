## Check yourself

<Question
  client:visible
  lang="en"
  q="The history has 1,000 commits and we know only that the first is good and the latest is broken. About how many times must bisect ask us good or bad to find the culprit?"
  options={['About 1,000, because it must look at every one', 'About 10, because it halves the range each time', 'Just once']}
  answer={1}
  why="Bisect cuts away half of what is left with every answer. If the range is N long it takes about log2 of N answers, which for 1,000 is about 10. The exercise confirms it with 16 commits and 4 answers."
/>

</Step>

<Step kind="question">

<Question
  client:visible
  lang="en"
  q="A test gives random results, passing sometimes and failing sometimes. What effect does that have on bisect?"
  options={['None', 'Bisect may point at the wrong commit, because some good or bad answers do not match the truth', 'Bisect works faster']}
  answer={1}
  why="Bisect believes every answer. If some answers are wrong, the ranges it cuts away are wrong too and it ends at a wrong commit. So the test should give the same result every time it runs on the same commit."
/>

</Step>
