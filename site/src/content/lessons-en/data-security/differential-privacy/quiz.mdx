## Check yourself

<Question
  client:visible
  lang="en"
  q="What does differential privacy at epsilon 1 guarantee about Ann?"
  options={['The attacker can learn nothing at all about Ann', 'The answers released have probabilities that differ by at most 2.718 times whether or not Ann is in the table, so somebody who sees an answer can tell the two worlds apart only to a limited degree', 'The table is encrypted when epsilon is 1']}
  answer={1}
  why="Differential privacy guarantees a bound on how much the distribution of the answers differs. It does not guarantee that the attacker learns nothing (for example he learns the share of patients in the whole group). The exercise works out that at epsilon 1 the highest ratio is exactly 2.718."
/>

</Step>

<Question
  client:visible
  lang="en"
  q="Why is the sensitivity of a count of people equal to 1?"
  options={['Because we count one person at a time', 'Because adding or removing one person changes the count by at most 1', 'Because epsilon is 1']}
  answer={1}
  why="Sensitivity is the most that the answer can change when the tables differ by one person. For a count it is 1. For a mean of n people clipped to a range of width 60,000 it is 60,000 divided by n."
/>

</Step>

  q="Why must a mean salary be clipped first?"
  options={['Because clipping makes the mean more accurate', 'Because without a limit one very high salary can move the mean enormously, the sensitivity has no bound, and no amount of noise can hide it', 'Because Laplace works only on numbers in a range']}
