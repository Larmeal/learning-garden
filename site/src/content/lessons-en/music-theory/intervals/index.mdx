## The interval is the basic unit of music theory

An **interval** is the distance between two notes. Scales, chords and melodies are all described with intervals. If you really know intervals, the rest of music theory is just putting pieces together.

The name of an interval has two parts:

1. **Number**: count the notes by letter, including the first and the last. C to E is a 3rd (C D E). C to G is a 5th. Ignore sharps and flats.
2. **Quality**: how wide or narrow it is within that number. The qualities are perfect, major, minor, augmented and diminished.

The quality comes from the real number of semitones. For the same number, the one that is a semitone wider is major and the narrower one is minor. For example, C to E (a 3rd) is 4 semitones wide and is called a major 3rd. C to E♭ is 3 semitones wide and is called a minor 3rd.

</Step>

## The table of twelve intervals

| Semitones | Short name | Full name | Example from C |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | P1 | perfect unison | C → C |
| 1 | m2 | minor 2nd | C → D♭ |
| 2 | M2 | major 2nd | C → D |
| 3 | m3 | minor 3rd | C → E♭ |
| 4 | M3 | major 3rd | C → E |
| 5 | P4 | perfect 4th | C → F |
| 6 | TT | tritone (aug 4th / dim 5th) | C → F♯ or G♭ |
| 7 | P5 | perfect 5th | C → G |
| 8 | m6 | minor 6th | C → A♭ |
| 9 | M6 | major 6th | C → A |
| 10 | m7 | minor 7th | C → B♭ |
| 11 | M7 | major 7th | C → B |
| 12 | P8 | perfect octave | C → C |

The numbers 1, 4, 5 and 8 are called **perfect** because they sound stable and have no major/minor forms. The numbers 2, 3, 6 and 7 have both major and minor forms. The tritone (an augmented 4th or a diminished 5th) sits exactly in the middle of the octave (6 semitones) and sounds the least stable.

**The name must be spelled correctly**: C → F♯ is an augmented 4th, and C → G♭ is a diminished 5th. These two sound the same, but they are written differently because of the letters (the 4th is F, the 5th is G).

</Step>

## Listen and look on the keyboard

Choose the lower note and the interval. Play the notes one after the other or together. Notice that the name of the upper note is always spelled by letter (for example, a major 7th up from F♯ gives E♯, not F).

## Inverting an interval

If you move the lower note up by one octave, the interval is **inverted**. There are two rules:

1. The numbers add up to 9 (a 3rd becomes a 6th, a 2nd becomes a 7th, a 4th becomes a 5th).
2. The qualities swap: major ↔ minor, perfect ↔ perfect, augmented ↔ diminished.

Example: C to E (a major 3rd) inverts to E to C (a minor 6th). The semitones always add up to 12 (4 + 8). You can use this as a quick check: if you know M3 = 4, then you know m6 = 8.

**Why it helps**: you only need to remember half of the table (up to the tritone), and the other half is the inversion.

</Step>

<Step>

## Intervals on the guitar

The guitar is an instrument where you see intervals as shapes. Every time you play the same shape in another position, it is the same interval.

| Interval | Shape on the neck |
|---|---|
| Semitone (m2) | Neighboring frets on one string |
| Whole tone (M2) | Skip one fret on one string |
| P4 | The same fret on neighboring strings (except string 3 and string 2, which give an M3) |
| M3 | The same fret between string 3 and string 2 |
| P5 | Neighboring strings, with the higher string two frets further up (for example string 6 fret 3 with string 5 fret 5) |
| P8 | Skip two strings and move two frets (or three frets when it crosses G–B), see the "[Fretboard Map](/en/guitar/fretboard/)" lesson |

The result is that all power chords (root and P5) are one shape that you can move along the whole neck. Every kind of triad also has a movable shape, because major and minor differ in the third (M3 or m3), which is a difference of one fret.

</Step>

1. Say M2 m3 M3 P4 P5 together with their semitone counts (2 3 4 5 7), quickly.
2. Pick any note and find its P5 and P4 (the P5 of A is E, the P4 of A is D).
3. Play fret 5 and then fret 8 on one string (m3), then alternate with fret 5 and then fret 9 (M3). Listen to the dark sound against the bright sound.
4. Listen to a song you like, find the first two notes of the melody, and try to name the interval.
