  q="You find 2.17% duplicate emails. The acceptable threshold is 5%. What is the result, and who should set the threshold?"
  options={['It passes, and the threshold should come from the data consumer who knows how much they can accept', 'It fails, because a good threshold is always 0%', 'It passes, and the data engineer should set the threshold entirely alone']}
  answer={0}
  why="2.17% does not exceed 5%, so it passes. The accepted threshold (acceptability) is a business agreement: the data consumer says how much is acceptable. There is no standard value, and the numbers in DMBOK2 Table 30 (page 480) are examples, not thresholds that work everywhere."

<Question
  lang="en"
  client:visible
  q="A metric goes out of range every month, but nobody has the authority or a way to fix it. According to DMBOK2's controllability principle, what should you do?"
  options={['Keep measuring, because data is always useful', 'Consider stopping the measurement, because a metric nobody can respond to is probably not useful', 'Increase the measurement frequency to every hour']}
  answer={1}
  why="DMBOK2 section 4.4 (pages 487 to 488) says a metric should reflect something that can be controlled. If it goes out of range it should prompt improvement, and if nobody can respond, the metric 'would probably not be useful'. From the rules side, DMBOK2 section 2.7.1 (page 478) also says that a measurement not tied to a business process should not be done."
/>

</Step>
