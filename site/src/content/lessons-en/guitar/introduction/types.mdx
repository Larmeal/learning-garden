## Grouped by how they make sound

| Family | The sound comes from | Needs an amp | Strings |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Acoustic** | The wooden resonating body | No | Nylon (classical) or steel (steel-string) |
| **Electric** | A pickup that sends a signal to an amp | Yes (or headphones or software) | Always steel |
| **Electro-acoustic** | The wooden body, plus a pickup (often a piezo) so you can plug into an amp or a PA | No, but you can connect one | Nylon or steel |

**Semi-hollow** and **hollow body** guitars are electric guitars with a hollow chamber inside, so they have an airy sound mixed with the sound from the pickups.

If you are not sure, choose by the music you most want to play, because wanting to play is the biggest source of motivation for learning.

</Step>

## Acoustic: sub-types by body shape

The size and shape of the body change the sound and how comfortable the guitar is to hold.

| Shape | Description | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| **Dreadnought** | Big body with wide shoulders, the most popular shape of steel-string acoustic | Loud and full, with strong bass, good for strumming |
| **OM / 000 (Auditorium)** | A little smaller | Balanced, clear for both strumming and fingerstyle |
| **Parlor** | Very small | Small, clear sound, good for old blues and travel |
| **Jumbo** | The biggest | Very loud, deep bass |
| **Classical** | Wide neck, flat top, nylon strings | Soft and warm, quieter than steel-string |
| **12-string** | Two strings for each note (some pairs are an octave apart) | A chorus-like, ringing sound |

**The classical neck is wider** (about 52 mm at the nut), so some chord shapes need a wider finger stretch. But nylon strings are softer, so they hurt the fingertips less than steel strings.

</Step>

## Electric: sub-types by body shape

| Group | Example idea | Sound and character |
|---|---|---|
| **Strat-style** | Curved body, three single-coil pickups, a tremolo arm | Clear, sharp, bright, light and comfortable |
| **Tele-style** | Flat body, two pickups, a fixed bridge | Clear with a snap, good for country and rock |
| **Les Paul-style** | Thick body, two humbuckers | Thick, full, long sustain |
| **Semi-hollow** | Body with a partly hollow chamber | Warm and spacious, good for blues and jazz |
| **Hollow body / archtop** | Fully hollow body | Soft and airy, the classic jazz sound |
| **Superstrat** | Like a Strat, but with a thinner neck and humbuckers | Designed for speed and heavy styles |
| **7-string, 8-string** | Extra strings for low notes | Low notes for metal and progressive |

The model names are trademarks of the makers, but "style" means a design that other makers have followed until it became a general type.

</Step>

## Pickups: what sets the character of the electric sound

| Type | Structure | Sound | Good to know |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Single-coil** | One coil | Clear, sharp, sparkling | You can hear a hum from the electricity around you, especially when the sound is distorted |
| **Humbucker** | Two coils wound in opposite directions | Thick, full | Cancels the hum. The name comes from "bucking the hum" |
| **P-90** | One coil, wider than a single-coil | Rough, warm | Sits between the two types above |
| **Piezo** | A crystal that responds to vibration instead of a magnet | Like an acoustic guitar | Found in electro-acoustic guitars. Works with nylon strings |

**Try it by ear** If your guitar has a pickup selector, play the same riff at the neck, middle (if there is one) and bridge positions. You will hear that the pickup near the neck sounds soft and round, and the pickup near the bridge sounds sharp and bright.

</Step>

## Strings and picks

**Types of strings**

- **Nylon** for classical guitars. The three high strings are plain nylon, and the three low strings are wound with metal.
- **Steel** for acoustic and electric guitars. The metal winding (for example phosphor bronze for acoustic and nickel for electric) gives a different tone.

**String gauge** is measured in thousandths of an inch. Common sets are:

| Set | String 1 to string 6 (example) | Character |
|---|---|---|
| Electric "9s" (light) | .009 .011 .016 .024 .032 .042 | Soft, easy to bend, good for beginners and for bending |
| Acoustic "12s" (light) | .012 .016 .024 .032 .042 .053 | Louder and tighter, needs more finger pressure |

Thick strings give a thick sound and more tension. Thin strings are easier to play but sound thinner. When you change the gauge, have a technician check the truss rod and the intonation.

**Picks** are about 0.46 mm (very thin) to 1.5 mm or more in thickness. A thin pick suits strumming. A heavy pick gives a sharper single-note sound and is easier to control. A small shape such as the **Jazz III** is popular for fast playing. No size is the one right answer, so try 2 or 3 kinds.

</Step>

## Choosing your first guitar or your next one

**Questions to answer before you buy**

1. **What music do you most want to play?** Folk, pop, singing along → acoustic. Rock, blues, jazz, metal → electric. Classical or bossa nova → classical.
2. **Are the strings too high?** If you need a lot of force to press fret 1, the cause is often the **action**, which a technician can adjust. It does not mean you are not good.
3. **Is the body too big?** A small guitar (parlor, 3/4) suits small people and children.
4. **Budget.** A mid-priced guitar with a good setup is easier to play than an expensive guitar with a bad setup.

**What to test yourself**

- Hold it and play sitting down. See if the neck feels comfortable.
- Press every fret on one string, one by one. Listen for buzzing or dead notes at any fret.
- Tune it, then press fret 12 and check that it matches the fret 12 harmonic (if it does not match, the intonation is wrong).

<Callout type="tip" title="For people who have played for a long time">
Before you buy a new guitar, try taking your current guitar for a setup (adjusting the action, truss rod and intonation, and changing the strings). Many people find that it plays so much more easily that it feels like a new guitar. If you still want to buy, say clearly what you need that your current guitar does not have, for example a clearer sound, a thinner neck, or a quiet sound for practicing at night.
</Callout>
