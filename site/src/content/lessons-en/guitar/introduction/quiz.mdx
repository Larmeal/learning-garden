<Question
  client:visible
  lang="en"
  q="You touch lightly above fret 12 and pluck the string, and you get a sound one octave higher. Why?"
  options={['The string gets tighter when the finger touches it', 'The finger stops the patterns that need the middle of the string to move, and what is left are the patterns with a node in the middle, the lowest of which is two arcs (k = 2)', 'The string gets shorter by half, just like pressing the fret']}
  answer={1}
  why="A light touch does not make the string shorter. It forces the middle point of the string to stay still. The patterns k = 1, 3, 5 need that point to move, so they are stopped. What is left is k = 2, 4, 6, which have a node in the middle, and k = 2 has twice the frequency."
/>

</Step>

  q="Why does touching lightly at fret 6 not give a clear harmonic?"
  options={['Because fret 6 is too close to the nut', 'Because the position of fret 6 is not close to a node of any pattern, so the finger stops every pattern', 'Because fret 6 needs a harder touch']}
  answer={1}

  options={['The string itself', 'The top (soundboard), which vibrates with the string through the bridge', 'The nut']}
  answer={1}
  why="A string is very thin, so it pushes very little air. Its vibration is passed through the saddle and the bridge to the top, which is wide and thin, vibrates as a plate and moves much more air than the string."
