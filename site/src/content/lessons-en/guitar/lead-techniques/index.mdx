## The same note can be played in different ways

If you play the exact same notes and pick every one of them straight, the sound is dry. The techniques of your left hand are what give notes different characters. Some notes connect, some glide, some change pitch. All the techniques below work on the pentatonic boxes you practiced in the previous lesson.

**Tab (tablature) symbols** used in this lesson

| Symbol | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| `h` | hammer-on | `5h8` |
| `p` | pull-off | `8p5` |
| `/` `\` | slide up / down | `5/7`, `7\5` |
| `b` | bend | `7b9` |
| `r` | release the bend | `7b9r7` |
| `~` | vibrato | `9~` |

## Hammer-on, pull-off and slides

**Hammer-on (h)**: pick the lower note, then "hammer" a finger down on a higher fret of the same string without picking again. The second note sounds because of the force of your finger, so the hammer must be fast and straight.

**Pull-off (p)** is the opposite. Pick the higher note, then "pluck" the fretting finger sideways (down toward the floor) a little, so that the string keeps vibrating on the lower note. Another finger must already be pressing that lower note.

Practice by alternating the two (a trill) at 60 bpm.

```text
B|-5h8p5h8p5h8p5-|     A minor pentatonic, string 2, frets 5 and 8
```

Every note must sound equally loud. If the second note is quieter, increase the speed of the finger that hammers or plucks. Do not press harder with the finger that stays down.

**Slide (/ \\)**: press and drag to the fret you want. Keep the pressure even and do not release until the slide ends.

```text
e|-5/7-5\3-|      slide up two frets, pick again at fret 5, then slide down two frets
```

The mistake most people make is not stopping exactly on the target fret. Practice by picking the target fret first, listening closely, then sliding to match that sound.

</Step>

## Bending to the target note

Bending is the technique that makes the guitar sing. But players who learn by ear often bend out of tune without knowing it, because they bend by feel and not toward a target note.

The principle: **bend until the sound matches the note on the higher fret**.

| Bend | Distance changed | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Half-step bend | equal to 1 fret | string 3, fret 7, bent to match fret 8 |
| Whole-step bend | equal to 2 frets | string 3, fret 7 (D), bent to match fret 9 (E) |

**Practice steps**

1. Pick the target note (fret 9, string 3) so your ear remembers the sound.
2. Move to fret 7 with your ring finger, and let your index and middle fingers push from behind on the same fret.
3. Bend slowly until the sound matches the target, then come back.
4. Repeat, alternating with the real target note to check. If the bent note is lower, bend more. If it is too high, release a little.

```text
G|-7b9-----7b9r7-----|     bend a whole step, hold, then release
```

The force comes from turning your wrist (like turning a door knob) plus the strength of your arm, not from one finger alone. One finger alone cannot bend well and risks injury. If you feel sharp pain in a joint, stop, as explained in the first lesson.

<Callout type="tip" title="Use your ear and a recording to check">
Record yourself while bending and listen back. While playing you often hear the note as correct, but in the recording it is clearly lower than the target. This works for every technique in this lesson.
</Callout>

## Vibrato: a shake that makes a note last

Vibrato is a small, repeated change of pitch up and down on a held note. It makes a long note "alive" and not stiff.

There are two basic kinds.

- **Wrist (rotational) vibrato**: press with the fingertip and turn your wrist like opening a door knob, pulling the string up and down at a right angle to the string. The sound is wide and thick. It is common in blues and rock.
- **Axial vibrato**: move the finger back and forth a little along the string. The sound is lighter. This is the kind used in classical guitar.

To practice, choose one note. Start slower than you would use in real playing (a few times per second) and keep it even. Then increase the width and speed. Add vibrato after the note has settled, not at the moment you pick it.

</Step>

## A good phrase needs space

Technique is a tool. The phrase is what people hear.

- **Sing before you play.** Try humming the phrase you want to play. If you cannot sing it in one breath, it is probably too long for the listener.
- **Leave space.** Play a phrase, then be silent, so the listener can keep up.
- **Repeat, then change a little.** The first phrase offers an idea. The second repeats it and changes the last note. The listener feels that there is a story.
- **End on a note you chose.** Do not stop only because you ran out of energy. Ending on the root or a chord tone makes the phrase feel finished.

**5-note exercise**: choose A minor pentatonic, box 1. Choose five notes from that shape and play short phrases with only those notes. Put one technique in each phrase. End on A every time. The limit makes you think about rhythm and technique, instead of running through notes without direction.

**This week's task**: choose one technique from this lesson (bending in tune is recommended). Practice it for 5 minutes a day. Record two phrases every day and compare them with yesterday's.
