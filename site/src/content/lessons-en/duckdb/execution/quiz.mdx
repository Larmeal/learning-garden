  q="We ask for the result of `select * from orders` (one million rows) one vector at a time. How many chunks do we get and how big are they?"
  options={[
    '1 chunk of one million rows',
    '489 chunks: 488 of 2,048 rows and one of 576 rows',
    '1,000,000 chunks of one row each',
    '9 chunks, one per row group'
  ]}
  answer={1}
  why="A scan hands data over as vectors of 2,048 rows, and 488 × 2,048 + 576 = 1,000,000. The 9 is the number of row groups of this table, which is the unit of storage and not the unit of passing data on (ran)."

<Step kind="question">

<Question
  client:visible
  lang="en"
  q="In the plan of `select sum(qty * unit_price) from orders where qty >= 3`, where is the condition `qty >= 3`?"
  options={[
    'In a separate FILTER operator above the scan',
    'In the top aggregate',
    'Inside the scan itself, so the scan returns only the 600,389 rows that pass',
    'Nowhere, because an aggregate needs no filter'
  ]}
  answer={2}
  why="The plan has three operators, UNGROUPED_AGGREGATE, PROJECTION and SEQ_SCAN. The scan returned 600,389 rows, the real number of rows with qty >= 3, because the optimizer moved the filter into the scan (ran). The optimizer lesson goes deeper into this."
/>

</Step>

<Question

  lang="en"
  q="A table of 9 row groups runs a heavy query at `threads = 8` and gets a CPU ÷ wall time of about 6.4. What does the number say?"
  options={[
    'The query used the CPU six times more slowly',
    'On average about 6 threads were working at the same time throughout the query',
    'The result is wrong because CPU time should not exceed wall time',
    'It spilled to disk 6 times'
  ]}
  answer={1}
  why="CPU time is the sum of the time of all threads. With one thread it equals the wall time (1.0), and with several threads working at once it can be above 1. In the exercise the nine-row-group table gave 6.4 at 8 threads and the one-row-group table gave 1.0 (ran)."
/>

</Step>
