<Question
  client:visible
  lang="en"
  q="One float column holds 1.0, NaN, 3.0 and another holds 1.0, null, 3.0. What are their averages?"
  options={['The first gives NaN and the second gives 2.0', 'Both give 2.0, because both special values are skipped', 'The first gives 2.0 and the second gives NaN']}
  answer={0}
  why="null is a missing value, and aggregate functions skip it (4 divided by 2 gives 2.0). NaN is a kind of number, so it is not skipped and spreads to the whole result. If your data has NaN mixed in, turn it into null first with fill_nan(None)."
/>

</Step>

<Question

  q="You call sort() with no options on a column that holds 3, null, 1. What is the result?"
  options={['null, 1, 3, because null is placed first by default', '1, 3, null, because null is always last', 'An error, because a missing value cannot be sorted']}
  answer={0}
  why="The default of sort puts null first. To put it last, pass nulls_last=True. Watch for this when you use the result for a top-k or a head."
