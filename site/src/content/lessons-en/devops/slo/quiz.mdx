## Check yourself

<Question
  client:visible
  lang="en"
  q="Which answer describes SLI, SLO and error budget best?"
  options={['The SLI is the target, the SLO is the measured number, and the error budget is the penalty', 'The SLI is the measured number (for example the share of successful requests), the SLO is its target (for example 99.9%), and the error budget is 1 minus the SLO, the amount that may fail', 'All three words mean the same thing']}
  answer={1}
  why="An SLI measures, an SLO sets the target, and the error budget is what is left of the target (1 minus 99.9% is 0.1%). Penalties belong to the SLA, which is the contract with the customer."
/>

</Step>

<Step kind="question">

<Question
  client:visible
  lang="en"
  q="Why did the alert rule 'more than 5% errors in one minute' wake people at night 24 times in a month in the exercise?"
  options={['Because the system breaks more often at night', 'Because at night there are few requests (about 8 a minute), so a single failure is already over 5%, although the overall budget hardly moved', 'Because Prometheus misbehaves at night']}
  answer={1}
  why="The rule looks only at the share within a minute, not at how much budget is spent. One failed request at night looks like 12% but eats very little budget. The burn rate is the opposite: it is tied to the budget of the whole month."
/>

</Step>

<Question

  q="A burn rate of 14.4 for one hour eats how much of the budget of 30 days?"
  options={['14.4%', '2% (14.4 times 1 hour, divided by the 720 hours of 30 days)', '50%']}
  answer={1}
  why="A burn rate of 1 uses up the budget exactly in 30 days (720 hours), so a burn rate of 14.4 for one hour eats 14.4 over 720, which is 2%. The exercise checked this arithmetic (ran)."
