## Check yourself

<Question
  client:visible
  lang="en"
  q="In the incident of the exercise, which stretch is the time to detect?"
  options={['From the deploy to the bell (4 minutes)', 'From the deploy until back to normal (21 minutes)', 'From the bell to somebody picking up (5 minutes)']}
  answer={0}
  why="Detect is from when the problem starts until the system knows, four minutes in the exercise. The stretch from the bell to somebody picking up is time to acknowledge, and the stretch from the start to back to normal is time to mitigate."
/>

</Step>

<Step kind="question">

<Question
  client:visible
  lang="en"
  q="The same bug: with no alert it ate 34% of the month's budget, with an alert and a one-command rollback 9%. Which statement explains the difference best?"
  options={['The second team writes better code', 'The difference is how soon the problem is known and how short the way back is, which sets how many minutes requests fail', 'The second team uses more servers']}
  answer={1}
  why="In the exercise the bug is the same, and 20% of the requests fail equally in every minute. But the first way fails until minute 45 and the last until minute 12. The damage is the failure rate times the time, as in [lessons 5](/en/devops/release/) and [9](/en/devops/slo/)."
/>

</Step>

<Step kind="question">

<Question
  client:visible
  lang="en"
  q="Where do the times that people take in the exercise (5 minutes to look, 10 to decide) come from?"
  options={['Measured on many real teams', 'They are assumptions of the exercise, set by the author and not measured', 'They are read from The DevOps Handbook']}
  answer={1}
  why="The times of the deploy and the rollback come from tags in Git, and the time of the bell comes from the burn rate rule. The times of people are recorded by no tool, so the exercise assumes them and says so. The final numbers are an example, not a value to expect in a real team."
/>

</Step>

<Question
  client:visible
  lang="en"
  q="Which is the better action item in the post-incident report of the exercise?"
  options={['Remind developers to be more careful', 'Add a test for requests that use a coupon, and release search changes to 10% of the traffic first (owner: the search team)', 'Ask whoever deployed to write a statement']}
  answer={1}
  why="A good action item fixes a gap in the system (no test, release to everything at once) and is owned by a team. The other two shift the burden onto a person, which goes against the blameless approach and does not prevent the same thing happening to somebody else."
